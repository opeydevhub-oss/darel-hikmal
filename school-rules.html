Introduction

These rules and regulations promote discipline, safety, academic excellence, good character, mutual respect, and a positive learning environment at DAR'EL-HIKMAH INTERNATIONAL ACADEMY. They apply, as relevant, to all pupils, parents or guardians, teaching staff, non-teaching staff, visitors, contractors, and other persons acting for or visiting the school.

All members of the school community must read, understand, and comply with these regulations and related school policies. The school may review and amend these regulations from time to time, subject to applicable law and any required approval or notice. The proprietor or school administration must approve amendments, and the school must give reasonable notice before they take effect, except where an immediate change is required by law or necessary to protect safety. The policy owner must coordinate scheduled reviews, maintain the current approved version, and ensure that approved amendments are communicated to the school community.

1. General Conduct

  1. All pupils, parents, guardians, staff, and visitors must treat one another with respect, courtesy, fairness, and dignity.
  2. Bullying, harassment, intimidation, discrimination, fighting, abusive language, threats, and violence are prohibited.
  3. Every member of the school community must act in a way that does not unlawfully or materially harm the Academy's good name, safety, or reputation.
  4. Honesty, responsibility, modesty, cleanliness, punctuality, and respect for reasonable authority are expected at all times.
  5. No person may damage, misuse, take, or retain school property or another person's belongings without permission.
  6. Lost property found on school premises must be given to the school office or another designated staff member.
  7. Pupils must follow reasonable and lawful instructions from teachers, administrators, and authorised staff.
  8. Visitors must report to the school office, state the purpose of their visit, follow visitor procedures, and comply with reasonable directions while on school premises. The school office or designated administrator may restrict or end a visit when reasonably necessary for safety, safeguarding, privacy, or orderly operation. The visitor must be told the reason where practicable and may raise a concern with the head of school or proprietor.

2. Attendance and Punctuality

  1. Pupils must attend school regularly and arrive on time, subject to lawful absence, approved leave, illness, or other circumstances accepted by the school administration.
  2. Parents or guardians must promptly notify the school when a pupil is absent or expected to arrive late and must give the reason when reasonably requested.
  3. An absence due to illness may require a medical report or other reasonable evidence, subject to applicable privacy and legal requirements. The head of school or designated administrator must decide whether evidence is required, apply the requirement consistently, and notify the parent or guardian of any available review or appeal route.
  4. Pupils must not leave school premises during school hours without prior approval from the school administration and release to an authorised parent, guardian, or other person approved under school procedures.
  5. Parents or guardians collecting pupils during school hours must report to the school office and complete the approved sign-out procedure.
  6. Staff members must report for duty punctually and remain available during assigned working hours, subject to approved leave or other authorised absence.
  7. Repeated lateness or unexplained absence may lead to a meeting with the parent or guardian and, where appropriate, proportionate disciplinary or administrative action under school policy and applicable law. The head of school or designated attendance officer must give reasonable notice of the concern, consider any explanation or mitigating circumstance, and provide any review or appeal procedure required by policy.

3. School Uniform and Appearance

  1. Pupils must wear the complete, clean, and properly maintained school uniform on all designated school days, unless the head of school or designated administrator approves an exception for a stated reason.
  2. School shoes, socks, sportswear, and other required items must be suitable for school activities and kept safe and serviceable.
  3. Pupils must maintain a neat, modest, and well-groomed appearance consistent with the school's stated Islamic and moral values.
  4. Unauthorised jewellery, make-up, extreme hairstyles, artificial nails, and other accessories that create a safety concern or materially disrupt learning are not permitted. The head of school or designated administrator must decide whether an item falls within this restriction using published or reasonably communicated standards, give notice to the pupil or parent where practicable, and provide a review route for disputed decisions.
  5. Staff members must dress professionally, modestly, and appropriately for their duties.
  6. Parents and visitors are expected to dress decently and respectfully while on school premises.

4. Academic Responsibilities

  1. Pupils must attend all lessons, participate appropriately, complete assignments, and bring required learning materials.
  2. Homework and classwork must be completed and submitted by the stated deadlines, unless the relevant teacher or school administrator authorises an extension on reasonable academic or welfare grounds.
  3. Cheating, plagiarism, copying, impersonation, unauthorised collaboration, falsification, and other forms of academic dishonesty are prohibited.
  4. Pupils must handle textbooks, exercise books, computers, laboratory materials, and other learning resources responsibly and according to instructions.
  5. Parents or guardians should support pupils' learning by monitoring homework, reading, revision, attendance, and school communications.
  6. Parents must not complete pupils' assignments or encourage academic dishonesty.
  7. Staff members must prepare lessons appropriately, teach responsibly, assess pupils fairly, and maintain accurate academic records.
  8. Teachers must provide appropriate guidance and report material concerns about a pupil's academic progress, behaviour, health, or welfare through the appropriate school channels.

5. Islamic, Moral, and Character Development

  1. Pupils must be encouraged to practise good manners, truthfulness, respect, kindness, self-discipline, and responsibility.
  2. All members of the Academy must respect Islamic teachings, prayers, moral instruction, and stated school values, subject to applicable law.
  3. Pupils must participate respectfully in approved religious, moral, and character-building activities, subject to any lawful accommodation or exemption procedure. The head of school or designated administrator must decide accommodation or exemption requests under applicable law and school policy, give written reasons when a request is refused, and provide a review or appeal route.
  4. Insulting, mocking, or harassing any person because of their faith, religious practice, culture, or background is prohibited.
  5. Staff members and parents are expected to model the values and behaviour the school seeks to develop in pupils.

6. Communication and Parent Responsibilities

  1. Parents or guardians must provide accurate and reasonably current contact, medical, emergency, and authorised collection information and promptly notify the school of material changes.
  2. Parents must read school notices, respond to official communications when a response is requested, and attend meetings when reasonably invited.
  3. Concerns or complaints should be raised respectfully through the appropriate school channels.
  4. Parents must not confront, threaten, insult, harass, or attempt to discipline another pupil or staff member on school premises.
  5. Parents should inform the school of any condition, circumstance, or change that may reasonably affect a pupil's learning, behaviour, health, or safety.
  6. Parents must pay school fees and other properly approved charges by the stated deadlines, subject to the applicable fee policy and any agreed arrangements.
  7. If fees remain unpaid, the school may take lawful and proportionate administrative measures permitted by its fee policy and applicable law, after providing any notice or opportunity required by law or policy. The proprietor or designated finance administrator must make the decision, state the amount and basis of the debt, and give the parent or guardian a reasonable opportunity to respond or request review. The school must not unlawfully deny a pupil essential care, safety, or legally protected access to education.

7. Health, Safety, and Safeguarding

  1. The safety and welfare of pupils are shared responsibilities of the entire school community.
  2. Pupils must follow reasonable safety instructions, emergency procedures, and evacuation drills.
  3. Dangerous objects, weapons, explosives, flammable materials, illegal substances, alcohol, tobacco, and vaping products are prohibited on school premises, during school activities, and on school transport, except where the head of school or designated safety officer expressly authorises them for a legitimate educational or operational purpose. Any authorisation must be documented, limited to that purpose, and comply with applicable law.
  4. The school may administer or store medication only under its medication procedures and appropriate written instructions from a parent, guardian, or qualified medical professional. The designated health or safeguarding officer must decide whether the procedure has been satisfied, notify the parent or guardian of any refusal or limitation, and provide the review route stated in the medication policy. The school does not assume responsibility for administering medication outside those procedures.
  5. Parents must not send a pupil to school with a contagious illness or when the pupil is medically unfit to attend and must follow applicable health guidance.
  6. Anyone who knows, suspects, or receives information indicating that a pupil may be experiencing abuse, neglect, exploitation, grooming, harassment, violence, self-harm risk, or another safeguarding concern must report it immediately and, in any event, on the same working day to the designated safeguarding lead (DSL). If the DSL is unavailable or implicated, the report must be made directly to the head of school or proprietor. If there is an immediate risk of harm, the person must contact emergency services or the relevant statutory child-protection authority without waiting for internal approval and must then notify the DSL or head of school as soon as safely practicable. Reports must be recorded factually, dated, signed, and handled under the safeguarding procedure. No person may investigate the allegation personally, promise secrecy, confront the alleged perpetrator, or delay reporting to protect the school's reputation.
  7. Staff members must maintain appropriate professional boundaries with pupils and comply with all applicable child-protection, safeguarding, mandatory-reporting, whistleblowing, reporting, and information-sharing requirements. A staff member who is unsure whether conduct or information is reportable must report the concern and seek guidance from the DSL. Uncertainty must not delay reporting.
  8. Corporal punishment, humiliating or degrading treatment, abusive language, and all forms of abuse are prohibited.
  9. Pupils must not enter restricted areas or operate equipment without permission, appropriate instruction, and adequate supervision. The responsible teacher or designated safety officer must define restrictions, record permission where appropriate, and refer disputed access decisions to the school administration for review.
  10. Safeguarding information must be shared only with persons who have a legitimate need to know and only as reasonably necessary to protect a pupil, comply with a reporting duty, obtain professional support, conduct a fair process, or comply with law. Confidentiality cannot be promised where disclosure is necessary to prevent harm, make a statutory report, obtain medical or emergency assistance, investigate a concern, or comply with a lawful request. The school must explain these limits to the person making a report where safe and appropriate.

8. Food, Cleanliness, and the Environment

  1. Pupils must keep classrooms, toilets, playgrounds, and other school areas reasonably clean.
  2. Waste must be placed in the appropriate bins.
  3. Pupils must wash their hands and maintain good personal hygiene.
  4. Food and drinks may be consumed only in designated areas and must comply with applicable school health and safety requirements.
  5. Sharing food is discouraged where allergies, intolerances, religious requirements, or other medical conditions may be involved.
  6. The head of school or designated administrator may restrict chewing gum and unauthorised food or drinks for health, safety, cleanliness, or operational reasons. The restriction must be communicated in advance where practicable, applied consistently, and reviewed on request by the head of school or proprietor.
  7. Everyone must take reasonable care of school property, plants, furniture, and the environment.

9. Mobile Phones, Devices, and Internet Use

  1. Pupils may not bring mobile phones, smartwatches, tablets, cameras, or other electronic devices to school without prior written permission from the head of school or a designated administrator, or as otherwise authorised by school policy.
  2. An authorised device must be switched off, stored securely, or used only as directed by a teacher or authorised staff member.
  3. An unauthorised or improperly used device may be temporarily retained by an authorised staff member under school policy. Any retention, inspection, or return must be proportionate, documented where appropriate, and carried out in accordance with applicable law and privacy requirements. The staff member must notify the pupil and, where appropriate, the parent or guardian of the reason, expected duration, and review process. The device must be returned to the pupil or an authorised parent or guardian within a reasonable period, subject to the policy and circumstances. The head of school or designated administrator must review any disputed retention or inspection, with an appeal to the proprietor where provided by policy.
  4. Recording, photographing, or sharing images, videos, or audio of pupils, staff, visitors, or school activities without appropriate permission or another lawful basis is prohibited.
  5. Cyberbullying, inappropriate online communication, unauthorised access, hacking, malware, and misuse of school networks, accounts, or devices are prohibited.
  6. School internet and technology resources may be used only for educational, operational, or otherwise approved purposes. The information-technology administrator may restrict access when reasonably necessary for security or operational reasons, must document material restrictions, notify affected users where practicable, and provide review by the head of school or designated administrator.
  7. Parents and staff must respect privacy and must not publish school-related images or information without appropriate consent or another lawful basis, except where disclosure is required or permitted by law.

10. Transport and Collection of Pupils

  1. Pupils using school transport must follow reasonable instructions from the driver and supervising staff.
  2. Pupils must remain seated, wear seat belts where provided, and keep hands and objects inside the vehicle.
  3. Fighting, shouting, distracting the driver, threatening others, and damaging the vehicle are prohibited.
  4. Pupils may be released only to a parent, guardian, or other person authorised in writing or otherwise approved under the school's collection procedures.
  5. Parents must promptly inform the school of any change to collection arrangements and provide information reasonably required to verify the authorised person's identity.
  6. The designated transport officer or supervising administrator may delay or refuse release when the authorised collection procedure has not been followed or release would create a reasonable safety concern. The decision must be based on documented safety or verification grounds. The officer must take reasonable steps to contact the parent or guardian and arrange safe supervision. The parent or guardian must be informed of the reason as soon as practicable and may request review by the head of school or proprietor.

11. Discipline and Corrective Measures

  1. Discipline must be fair, reasonable, proportionate, age-appropriate, consistent with safeguarding obligations, and primarily directed toward correcting behaviour, protecting the school community, and promoting responsibility.
  2. Before imposing a disciplinary measure, the decision-maker must, where reasonably practicable, consider the nature and seriousness of the conduct, its impact, the pupil's age and circumstances, any relevant history, and any mitigating or aggravating factors.
  3. Depending on the circumstances, corrective or disciplinary measures may include:
    • Verbal correction, guidance, or counselling
    • A warning or written undertaking
    • Loss or restriction of specified privileges
    • Detention or supervised reflection, where lawful, safe, and appropriately supervised
    • Restitution or repair of damaged property, where appropriate and without imposing an unlawful or disproportionate financial penalty
    • Parent or guardian notification or meeting
    • A behaviour-monitoring or support plan
    • Temporary suspension under school policy and applicable law
    • Withdrawal of admission or expulsion in serious or repeated cases, following the applicable procedure and any required approval
    The relevant teacher may impose minor corrective measures within published school limits. The head of school or designated administrator must approve suspension, withdrawal of admission, or expulsion. The decision-maker must give notice of the allegation, provide a reasonable opportunity to respond, state the reasons and duration of the measure, and provide review or appeal rights under school policy and applicable law.
  4. A disciplinary measure must not be imposed solely because a pupil, parent, or staff member has made a good-faith complaint, appeal, safeguarding report, whistleblowing disclosure, or request for assistance.
  5. Serious misconduct may result in temporary removal from class or immediate suspension when reasonably necessary to protect safety, preserve order, or allow a fair investigation. The head of school or designated administrator must authorise or promptly review the action, notify the parent or guardian of the reason and expected duration, and provide an opportunity to respond and a review or appeal procedure. Such action is not a finding of guilt.
  6. Pupils must have a reasonable opportunity to explain their conduct before a final disciplinary decision is made, except when immediate action is necessary for safety or to prevent interference with an investigation. The school must provide written notice of the decision, reasons, duration, and any review or appeal rights required by its policy or applicable law.
  7. Parents or guardians are expected to cooperate with disciplinary, safeguarding, and welfare procedures.
  8. Staff misconduct must be handled by the authorised manager or proprietor under employment policies, applicable law, confidentiality requirements, and fair-process standards. The staff member must receive notice of the concern, an opportunity to respond, and any review or appeal rights required by law or policy. When the concern involves abuse, neglect, exploitation, harassment, retaliation, or another safeguarding matter, the safeguarding reporting route takes priority and must not be replaced by an internal disciplinary process.
  9. Corporal punishment, humiliating treatment, degrading language, collective punishment, and unlawful discrimination must not be used as disciplinary measures.
  10. No person may threaten, intimidate, disadvantage, dismiss, suspend, discipline, exclude, harass, or otherwise retaliate against a person who makes a good-faith report, complaint, appeal, safeguarding disclosure, or whistleblowing disclosure, or who participates in an investigation. Suspected retaliation must be reported immediately to the head of school or proprietor, or to the designated safeguarding authority when the concern involves safeguarding or the head of school.

12. Prohibited Conduct

The following conduct is prohibited, where applicable, on school premises, during school activities, on school transport, or through school-related communications:

  • Bullying, cyberbullying, harassment, or unlawful discrimination
  • Fighting, assault, threats, stalking, or intimidation
  • Theft, extortion, fraud, or deliberate damage to property
  • Possession, supply, or use of weapons, illegal drugs, alcohol, tobacco, or vaping products
  • Cheating, examination malpractice, plagiarism, impersonation, or falsification of records
  • Abusive, obscene, discriminatory, or threatening language
  • Unauthorised recording, photography, or publication of school-related content
  • Leaving school or a supervised activity without permission
  • Bringing dangerous, offensive, unlawful, or materially disruptive materials to school
  • Conduct that reasonably endangers another person's safety, dignity, privacy, or wellbeing

The head of school or designated administrator must determine alleged breaches through a fair process, including investigation, notice, an opportunity to respond, and the review or appeal procedures in Section 13 and applicable school policy.

13. Complaints and Appeals

  1. Complaints should first be directed respectfully to the class teacher, relevant staff member, designated safeguarding lead where appropriate, or school office. The recipient must acknowledge the complaint within a reasonable period, record it where appropriate, and refer it to the appropriate decision-maker. A safeguarding concern must be reported immediately under Section 7 and must not be treated only as an ordinary complaint.
  2. If the matter is not resolved, it may be referred to the school administration or proprietor under the school's complaints procedure. The procedure must identify the responsible decision-maker, applicable time limits, required notice, investigation steps, response period, and available review or appeal. A complaint about the head of school must be submitted directly to the proprietor or designated safeguarding authority. A complaint about the proprietor must be referred to the appropriate external regulator, statutory authority, or other independent body where required or available.
  3. Complaints must be made honestly and respectfully. The head of school or proprietor may take appropriate action in response to knowingly false, malicious, threatening, or abusive complaints while ensuring that good-faith concerns are not penalised. Before action is taken, the person concerned must receive notice, an opportunity to respond, written reasons, and any applicable review or appeal rights. A complaint must not be treated as malicious merely because it is unsubstantiated or not upheld.
  4. Parents and pupils may appeal disciplinary decisions through the approved school process and within any stated time limit. Where reasonably practicable, the appeal must be considered by someone who was not the original decision-maker. The appellant must receive a written outcome with reasons.
  5. The school must handle complaints, investigations, and appeals as confidentially as reasonably possible. Information may nevertheless need to be shared with persons who have a legitimate need to know, safeguarding or law-enforcement authorities, professional advisers, insurers, regulators, or others where disclosure is required or permitted by law. Confidentiality does not prevent mandatory reporting, emergency action, fair investigation, or disclosure necessary to protect a person from harm. The school must explain these limits to the complainant where safe and appropriate.
  6. No person may suffer retaliation for making or supporting a good-faith complaint, appeal, safeguarding report, whistleblowing disclosure, or request for assistance. Retaliation includes threats, intimidation, adverse treatment, unjustified disciplinary action, exclusion, dismissal, reduction of duties or opportunities, harassment, or any other disadvantage connected with the report or participation. Suspected retaliation must be reported immediately to the head of school or proprietor, or to the designated safeguarding authority where appropriate, and must be investigated separately.
  7. Safeguarding concerns must be handled under the safeguarding procedure and must not be delayed by ordinary complaint or appeal stages. A complaint concerning the head of school must be referred directly to the proprietor or designated safeguarding authority.
  8. The school must keep secure records of complaints, safeguarding reports, decisions, actions, and appeals, including dates, responsible persons, reasons, and outcomes, in accordance with applicable data-protection and retention requirements.

14. Staff Responsibilities

  1. Staff members must be punctual, professional, respectful, and committed to pupils' welfare, safety, and education.
  2. Staff must prepare adequately, teach effectively, assess fairly, and maintain appropriate classroom management.
  3. Staff must protect confidential information relating to pupils, families, staff, and the Academy. Confidential information may be accessed, used, or disclosed only for legitimate school purposes, safeguarding, legal compliance, mandatory reporting, emergency protection, or another lawful purpose, and only to the extent reasonably necessary. Confidentiality must not be used to conceal abuse, neglect, exploitation, misconduct, retaliation, or a risk of harm.
  4. Staff must promptly report safeguarding, health, safety, behavioural, and academic concerns through the appropriate channels. Any staff member who knows, suspects, or receives information indicating a safeguarding concern must report it immediately and, in any event, on the same working day to the DSL. If the DSL is unavailable or implicated, the report must be made directly to the head of school or proprietor. If the head of school or proprietor is implicated, the report must be made to the designated safeguarding authority or relevant statutory authority. Where there is an immediate risk of harm, staff must contact emergency services or the relevant statutory authority without waiting for internal approval. Safeguarding concerns must not be suppressed or delayed because of confidentiality, uncertainty, fear of consequences, or reputational considerations.
  5. Staff must not engage in corporal punishment, favouritism, harassment, exploitation, retaliation, or inappropriate relationships or communications with pupils.
  6. Staff must use school resources responsibly and comply with all applicable administrative, academic, health, safety, data-protection, and safeguarding policies.
  7. Staff must communicate with parents through approved and professional channels and maintain required records.
  8. Staff must cooperate honestly with safeguarding, disciplinary, complaint, and regulatory investigations, preserve relevant records and evidence, and must not coach witnesses, obstruct an investigation, destroy records, or contact an alleged victim or alleged perpetrator about a report except as authorised.
  9. Staff who make a good-faith report or participate in an investigation must not suffer retaliation. Any staff member who experiences or observes retaliation must report it to the head of school, proprietor, or appropriate external authority. Retaliation is a disciplinary matter and may also be reported to a statutory or regulatory authority where required.
  10. The DSL must maintain clear reporting routes; ensure that staff know how and when to report; acknowledge and record reports; make or coordinate referrals to statutory authorities where required; provide appropriate support and risk-management measures; and escalate unresolved or conflicted concerns to the proprietor or relevant external authority. The DSL must not close or downgrade a safeguarding concern solely to protect the Academy's reputation or avoid disciplinary consequences.

15. Agreement and Compliance

By enrolling a pupil, accepting employment, or entering the school premises, pupils, parents, guardians, staff, and visitors are expected to respect and comply with these rules and regulations to the extent applicable to them. Failure to comply may result in proportionate corrective, disciplinary, administrative, contractual, or legal action, subject to applicable law, school policy, and fair procedures. Anyone authorised to make a discretionary decision under these regulations must act within the stated limits, provide required notice, record material reasons, and follow the applicable review or appeal procedure. Nothing in these regulations limits any statutory right, safeguarding duty, reporting obligation, privacy right, whistleblowing protection, non-retaliation protection, or other legal protection. These regulations must be read together with the Academy's admission policies, fee policy, safeguarding policy, staff handbook, examination rules, transport procedures, data-protection and privacy procedures, complaints procedure, whistleblowing procedure, and other official school policies.

DAR'EL-HIKMAH International Academy is committed to maintaining a safe, respectful, disciplined, inclusive, and morally upright environment in which every pupil can learn, grow, and succeed.
